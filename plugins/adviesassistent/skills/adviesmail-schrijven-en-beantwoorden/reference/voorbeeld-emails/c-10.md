## Bericht 2 — Adviseur

Dear [adviesvrager],

Thank you for reaching out to Onderwijsloket with your questions, I’m happy to
think along.

If you want to work as a teacher in the Netherlands, you need a teaching
qualification or other suitable certification.You can obtain a teachers degree
by following a fulltime or parttime teacher training program at a research
university ([universiteit](https://www.onderwijsloket.com/kennisbank/artikel-archief/how-is-the-dutch-education-system-structured/)) or a university of
applied sciences
([hogeschool](https://www.onderwijsloket.com/kennisbank/artikel-archief/how-is-the-dutch-education-system-structured/)). However, foreign students need to
show that their Dutch is at B2 level, before applying for a bachelor’s degree.
You can read more about the Dutch language requirements in this
[article](https://www.onderwijsloket.com/kennisbank/artikel-archief/do-i-have-to-speak-dutch-in-order-to-become-a-teacher-in-the-netherlands/). A notable
exception is the bachelor ‘Teaching Education in English’, obtainable at the
Hogeschool Utrecht. This is the only teacher training program in the
Netherlands that doesn’t require an understanding of Dutch at B2 level. You
can find more information their Spanish program on [their
website](https://www.internationalhu.com/bachelor-programmes/teacher-education-in-spanish "https://www.internationalhu.com/bachelor-programmes/teacher-education-in-spanish").

The zij-instroom in beroep traject, is based on your previous educational
background. For this programme, you need the B2 level in Dutch and a place to
work in education.

If you have obtained a teaching degree in another country, you need to have
your degree certified. It is [DUO](https://duo.nl/particulier/buitenlands-diploma-in-nederland/werken-als-leraar.jsp) who certifies (erkent) teaching
qualifications obtained outside of the Netherlands. If necessary, DUO makes
use of the services of [Nuffic](https://www.nuffic.nl/) or
[SBB](https://www.s-bb.nl/). These institutions assess foreign diplomas by
comparing them to a Dutch equivalent. Nuffic assesses diplomas from secondary
education and higher education, while SBB assesses diplomas on
[mbo](https://www.onderwijsloket.com/kennisbank/artikel-archief/how-is-the-dutch-education-system-structured/) level. DUO is the only authority that can
certify your foreign teaching qualification.

Furthermore, you could work at an international school. At this type of
schools, you don’t necessarily need a Dutch teaching qualification, but you
would still need a teaching degree of some sort to show your teaching
abilities. International schools are private schools, so their qualification
requirements can differ from what is legally required in the Netherlands. You
can always contact a school to ask about their policy:
<https://www.dutchinternationalschools.nl/>.

For now, it is important to contact DUO and have you teaching qualification
certified. From there, you will know if you need extra schooling and how
much.

I hope this provides an answer to your question. Feel free to contact us again
if you have any further questions! We also have the option to plan a (free)
English consulatation, you can find it though [this
link](https://onderwijsloket.com/english "https://onderwijsloket.com/english").
